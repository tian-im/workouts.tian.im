Read this first · two minutes

Six rules, then you can start.

Nothing here is complicated, but none of it is optional either. Read once, tap the button at the bottom, and the sessions unlock.

01

Compound first — no exceptions

The first lift of every session is a barbell compound: bench, squat, row, deadlift, pull-up. It gets your freshest effort, the longest rest (3–4 min) and never goes to failure. Everything after it is support work.

02

It's a rotation, not a calendar

Train A → B → C → D → E → rest → repeat. If life eats Tuesday, you don't restart the week — you do the next letter on the next day you show up. The rotation is the schedule.

03

Stop two reps short of failure

On compounds, finish each set with ~2 reps left in the tank (RIR 2). On isolation work, 1 rep left is fine. Grinding to failure every set wrecks your next session and your form.

04

Add weight by the rule, not by feel

When all sets hit the top of the rep range with 2 reps in reserve, add 2.5 kg upper body or 5 kg lower body next time. Not before.

05

Back gets touched three times a week

Once heavy and horizontal (Day C), once vertical for width (Day E), once as support on push day (Day A). That frequency is why the back grows faster than anything else on this plan.

06

Log every set

Weight and reps. Progressive overload is the only mechanism that actually builds muscle — and you can't beat last month if you don't know what last month was.

The one non-negotiable

Never skip Day C.

If the week collapses to three sessions, they are A, C and D. Push, back, hinge. That trio keeps strength moving and still hits the back hard enough to grow.

First session only

Find your starting weights

Don't guess. On each main lift, ramp up in small jumps until a set of 5–6 feels like you have 3 reps left. That's your Week 1 weight. It will feel too easy — good.

Bar × 10 · 40% × 5 · 60% × 3 · 80% × 1 · work sets

Coming back from being sick

Week 0: ramp back

  • →Train only after 24 h symptom-free.
  • →Two sessions: A and C. Two sets each, 50–60% load, stop at RIR 4.
  • →No deadlifts, no AMRAP. Then start Week 1 from the top.

Session A · Upper push

Push — Bench strength

Horizontal pressing power, then shoulder and triceps volume. The chest-supported row at the end feeds the back goal and keeps your shoulders from rounding forward.

~70 min
0 / 18

Warm-up   5 min rower or bike · band pull-aparts 2×15 · push-ups ×10 · ramp sets on bench.

01

Barbell Bench Press

Shoulder blades pinned back and down. Bar to lower chest, elbows ~45° from your body. Drive your feet into the floor.

3 × 5–6·RIR 2·3-1-1-0·rest 3 min
kg
02

Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press

Bench upright, ribs down. Press until the dumbbells nearly touch, without flaring your lower back off the pad.

3 × 8–10·RIR 2·rest 2 min
kg
03

Incline Dumbbell Press

Bench at 30°. Let the dumbbells drift out for a real stretch at the bottom, then press up and slightly in.

3 × 10–12·RIR 1–2·rest 90 s
kg
04

Chest-Supported Row

Chest glued to the pad, no body english. Pull elbows past your ribs and hold one second at the top. This is back volume, not ego lifting.

3 × 12–15·RIR 1·1 s squeeze·rest 75 s
kg
05

Cable Lateral Raise

Lead with the elbow, stop at shoulder height. Keep the cable behind your body so there's tension even at the bottom.

3 × 15–20·RIR 0–1·rest 60 s
kg
06

Rope Triceps Pushdown

Elbows pinned to your sides, spread the rope at the bottom. Upper arms don't move.

3 × 12–15·RIR 1·rest 60 s
kg

Cool-down   Doorway chest stretch 2×30 s/side · overhead triceps stretch 2×30 s/side.

Session B · Lower

Squat — lower strength

Heavy squat, then quad and hamstring volume. This is the day that makes everything else feel lighter.

~70 min
0 / 19

Warm-up   5 min bike · leg swings 10/side · bodyweight squats ×15 · hip airplanes 8/side · ramp sets.

01

Back Squat

Brace, break at the hips and knees together, knees track over toes. Depth you can own — no bouncing out of the hole.

3 × 5–6·RIR 2·3-1-1-0·rest 3–4 min
kg
02

Leg Press

Feet mid-platform, lower until your lower back starts to tuck, then press without locking out hard.

3 × 10–12·RIR 2·rest 2 min
kg
03

Leg Extension

Slow up, slow down, squeeze the quad at the top. No swinging the stack.

3 × 12–15·RIR 1·2-1-1-1·rest 60 s
kg
04

Seated Leg Curl

Toes pulled up, drive the pad down and back. Pause at the fully bent position — that's where the hamstring works hardest.

3 × 12–15·RIR 1·rest 75 s
kg
05

Standing Calf Raise

Full stretch at the bottom, 2 s pause at the top. Calves need the pause, not more weight.

4 × 8–10·RIR 1·2 s pause·rest 60 s
kg
06

Hanging Knee Raise

Curl your pelvis up, don't just swing your legs. Slow on the way down.

3 × 10–15·RIR 1·rest 60 s
kg

Cool-down   Couch stretch 2×45 s/side · pigeon 2×45 s/side · quad stretch 2×30 s/side.

Session C · Priority day

Back — thickness

The whole point of the program. Heavy horizontal rowing first, then loaded stretch, traps and rear delts. Do this day fresh, not at the end of the week.

~85 min
0 / 23

Warm-up   5 min rower · scap pulldowns 2×10 · band pull-aparts 2×15 · empty-bar rows ×10 · ramp sets.

01

Barbell Row

Hinge to ~30–45°, brace, pull to your lower ribs. Torso stays still — if you have to heave, the weight is too heavy. Straps are allowed here.

4 × 5–6·RIR 2·2-0-1-1·rest 3 min
kg
02

Seal Row / Chest-Supported T-Bar Row

Chest supported removes the lower back entirely, so the mid-back has to do all the work. Pull elbows back and down, pause 1 s.

4 × 8–10·RIR 1–2·1 s squeeze·rest 2 min
kg
03

Seated Cable Row — Neutral Grip

Let the shoulder blades travel forward at the start, then pull them back past neutral. Chest tall, no rocking.

3 × 10–12·RIR 1·1 s hold·rest 90 s
kg
04

Straight-Arm Pulldown

Lats only — arms stay almost straight. Reach forward for the stretch, drive down with your armpits. This is the loaded-stretch lift.

3 × 12–15·RIR 1·2-1-1-1·rest 60 s
kg
05

Barbell Shrug

Straight up, 2 s hold at the top, controlled down. No rolling. Use straps so grip isn't the limiting factor.

3 × 10–12·RIR 1·2 s hold·rest 60 s
kg
06

Face Pull

Rope at eye height, pull to your forehead and rotate your hands back. Light weight, high reps. This is shoulder insurance.

3 × 15–20·RIR 1·rest 60 s
kg
07

Reverse EZ-Bar Curl

Trains the brachialis, which pushes the biceps up and adds arm thickness from the side. Wrists neutral, elbows still.

3 × 10–12·RIR 1·rest 60 s
kg

Running long?   Drop #7 and #6 — never drop #1 or #2. They're the reason this day exists.

Cool-down   Lat stretch on the rack 2×30 s/side · child's pose 60 s · doorway chest stretch.

Session D · Lower

Hinge — deadlift strength

Pull from the floor, then hamstrings, single-leg work and erectors. Deadlift is a skill: reset every rep, and stop the set when your back rounds.

~75 min
0 / 19

Warm-up   5 min bike · dowel hip hinge ×10 · glute bridge ×15 · dead hang 20 s · ramp sets.

01

Deadlift — Conventional or Trap Bar

Bar over mid-foot, slack out of the bar, push the floor away. Reset your setup between reps. If the bar drifts forward, stop the set.

3 × 4–5·RIR 3·reset each rep·rest 3–4 min
kg
02

Romanian Deadlift

Soft knees, hips back, bar shaving the thighs. Stop when your hamstrings stretch — not when the bar hits the floor.

3 × 8–10·RIR 2·3-0-1-0·rest 2 min
kg
03

Bulgarian Split Squat

Front foot far enough forward that the knee stays over the mid-foot. Torso upright. This one will humble you — start light.

3 × 8–10 / leg·RIR 2·rest 90 s
kg
04

Lying Leg Curl

Hips down on the pad, curl all the way, lower over 3 s. Different knee angle than the seated version — that's why both are here.

3 × 12–15·RIR 1·3-0-1-0·rest 60 s
kg
05

45° Back Extension

Brace and extend through the hips, not by rounding and snapping the spine. This builds the erectors that make a back look thick from the side.

3 × 12–15·RIR 1·rest 60 s
kg
06

Seated Calf Raise

Knees bent hits the soleus. Slow reps, full stretch at the bottom, no bouncing.

4 × 12–15·RIR 1·rest 45 s
kg

Cool-down   Hamstring stretch 2×45 s/side · hip flexor stretch 2×30 s/side.

Session E · Upper pull

Width — lats & arms

Vertical pulling for lat width, plus arms and rear delts. Width comes from the lats flaring out — thickness from Day C. You need both.

~70 min
0 / 21

Warm-up   5 min rower · dead hang 2×20 s · scap pull-ups 2×8 · band pull-aparts 2×15 · ramp sets.

01

Weighted Pull-Up or Lat Pulldown

Start from a dead hang, pull elbows to your ribs, chin over the bar. Can't do 6 clean reps? Use the lat pulldown and earn the pull-up.

4 × 6–8·RIR 1–2·rest 2–3 min
kg
02

One-Arm Dumbbell Row

Brace a hand on the bench, pull the dumbbell toward your hip, let the shoulder blade travel. No torso twist.

3 × 8–10 / arm·RIR 2·rest 90 s
kg
03

Wide-Grip Lat Pulldown

Lighter than #1, wider grip, slower negative. Chase the stretch at the top and the squeeze at the bottom — this is a shaping lift.

3 × 12–15·RIR 1·3-1-1-1·rest 75 s
kg
04

Incline Dumbbell Curl

Bench at 45–60°, arms hang behind you. That position stretches the long head — more range, more growth.

3 × 10–12·RIR 1·rest 60 s
kg
05

Cable Overhead Triceps Extension

Rope behind your head, elbows pointing forward. The overhead position loads the long head — the one that adds size.

3 × 10–12·RIR 1·rest 60 s
kg
06

Rear-Delt Fly

Reverse pec deck or cable. Lead with the elbows, stop at shoulder height. Rear delts fill the gap between your shoulders and your traps.

3 × 15–20·RIR 0–1·rest 60 s
kg
07

Dead Hang

Just hang. Decompresses the spine, builds grip and shoulder health. Breathe.

2 × max·rest 60 s
kg

How the week fits together

Then repeat

If you get 6 days

Add a Day F: pump + conditioning — 30–40 min of lat pulldown 3×12, cable fly 3×12, lateral raise 3×15, curls 3×12, calves 3×15, then 15 min easy cardio. High reps, no grinding.

If you get 4 days

A, B, C, D. You drop the dedicated width day, so add one extra set of lat pulldown to the end of Day C. The back still gets three touches.

If you get 2–3 days

A, C, D — push, back, hinge. Do them in that order, never two in one day. Two sessions a week still maintains; three builds.

The rules for skipping

  • Missed a day? Do the next letter. Don't restart, don't double up.
  • More than 10 days off? One ramp-back session at 60% weight and half the sets.
  • Never put Day C and Day E back-to-back if you can help it — both are heavy pulling.
  • Never train the same heavy lift on consecutive days. That's the whole reason for the rotation.

The 8-week block

More weight, or more reps. Never neither.

01–02

Weeks 1–2

Technique

Same exercises, lighter loads (RIR 3–4). Log everything. The goal is repeatable reps, not numbers.

03–06

Weeks 3–6

Build

RIR 2. Double progression: add reps inside the range, then add weight and reset to the bottom of the range.

07

Week 7

Deload

Same weights, half the sets, nothing near failure. You'll feel ridiculous. Do it anyway — that's when the growth catches up.

08

Week 8

Test

On the five main lifts, take your Week 3 weight and do one set to technical failure. Recalculate and start the next block +2.5–5 kg.

Double progression, in practice

Bench is written as 3 × 5–6. You start at 60 kg:

Week 360 kg × 5, 5, 5
Week 460 kg × 6, 6, 5
Week 560 kg × 6, 6, 6
Week 662.5 kg × 5, 5, 5

Three sets at the top of the range = permission to add weight. Not before, not by feel.

What "RIR 2" actually means

Reps In Reserve: how many clean reps you could still do when you rack the bar. RIR 2 means the set felt hard but you had two left.

RIR 3–4

Weeks 1–2. Deliberately easy. You're learning the movement.

RIR 2

Compounds, weeks 3–6. Hard but never a grind.

RIR 0–1

Isolation and machines only. Curls, raises, pushdowns.

If your form changes on the last rep, the set is over — that's failure regardless of the number.

Goal two, in detail

A thicker back is four things, not one exercise.

"Thick" means the back has depth from the side — lats, mid-back, traps and erectors all contributing. That's why Day C has seven exercises and why pulling appears on three days.

01

Heavy rowing

Barbell row, seal row. Heavy tension through the mid-back and rhomboids. This is the thickness driver — it gets the first half of Day C.

02

Loaded stretch

Straight-arm pulldown, wide-grip pulldown, pull-ups. Lats respond to being stretched under load — not just squeezed.

03

Traps & erectors

Shrugs and back extensions. These are what people see behind you and from the side. Most programs leave them out. This one doesn't.

04

Frequency

Three back touches a week, ~27 direct sets. The back recovers faster than legs, so it can take the frequency — and frequency beats one big session.

What to expect, honestly

Month 1

Better posture, stronger pulls, numbers climbing fast. Mirror probably looks the same — that's normal.

2–3 mo

Shoulders sit back, upper back looks fuller in a fitted shirt. Photos start showing real difference.

4–6 mo

Visible thickness from the side and back. This is where people start asking what you've been doing.

None of that happens at maintenance calories. Muscle needs material — see the fuel notes below.

The four lifts that matter most

If you only track four numbers for the next six months, track these:

  • Barbell RowDay C
  • Weighted Pull-UpDay E
  • Bench PressDay A

Add 5 kg to the row and 2.5 kg to the pull-up over the block and the back will look different. Not because of a secret exercise — because of arithmetic.

Support

Eat enough, sleep enough, or nothing above works.

Fuel

What to eat

  • →Protein: 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight per day. If you're 75 kg, that's 120–165 g, spread over 3–4 meals.
  • →Calories: 200–300 above maintenance. Enough to build, slow enough to stay lean.
  • →Timing: protein within a couple of hours of training, carbs around it. Total daily intake matters more than the exact window.

Recovery

The other half of the work

  • →Sleep: 7–9 h. Below 6 h and strength gains roughly halve. Not a soft recommendation.
  • →Cardio: 2–3 × 20–30 min zone 2 on rest days. Doesn't interfere; helps recovery.
  • →Stress: if a week is chaos, keep the compounds and cut the isolation. Half a session beats none.

Non-negotiable

Training hard is the easy part. Showing up 3× a week for 24 weeks is the whole game. Everything on this page is designed so a bad, busy, tired week still counts as a win.

Questions

The usual ones

Can I move exercises around?

Yes, as long as you keep the movement pattern and keep the first lift first. No barbell row? Use a chest-supported row. No pull-up? Lat pulldown. Don't replace a compound with a curl.

How do I know my starting weight?

Ramp to a set of 5–6 with ~3 reps left. If in doubt, go lighter. You'll pass that weight in two weeks anyway, and bad form now costs you a month.

What if I miss a week?

Pick up where you left off in the rotation, drop each main lift's weight by ~10% for one session, then continue. Don't restart the block.

Will this make me bulky?

Not accidentally. You'd need years of eating in a surplus. At 200–300 kcal over maintenance you'll add size slowly and stay lean — exactly what you want for a thicker back.

Why no direct ab work every day?

You get hanging knee raises on B and heavy bracing on every compound. If you want more, add 2 sets of cable crunches at the end of A and E.

Do I need to go to failure?

No. RIR 1–2 builds the same muscle with far less fatigue. Failure is for the Week 8 test and the occasional isolation set — not every session.

What about cardio?

2–3 sessions of 20–30 min zone 2 on rest days. Keep it separate from lifting where possible, and don't replace a lifting day with it.
